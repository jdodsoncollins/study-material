# How AI interview policies changed from 2025 through 2026

## Snapshot

- Early 2025 was about bans and detection as companies reacted to AI-assisted cheating
- Mid-2026 brought AI-assisted rounds added to existing loops at big companies
- Late 2026 saw AI fluency become a written hiring requirement, with some companies dropping algorithm rounds entirely
- The industry remains split: about one-third ban AI, one-third allow it with constraints, one-third vary by round
- Interview transcripts from AI assistants became graded artifacts during 2026

## Why it shows up in interviews

Understanding when policies changed helps you recognize what recruiters mean by "AI-allowed" versus "AI-expected." A company that added an AI round in early 2026 may still forbid AI in its other rounds. The timeline shows the shift from reactive bans to deliberate AI-fluency testing.

## Core idea

### The three phases

**2025: Bans and detection**
Companies reacted to candidates using ChatGPT and coding assistants by:
- Publishing explicit no-AI policies with behavioral tells to watch for
- Building detection into proctoring platforms (screenshots, typing patterns, overlay detection)
- Reintroducing in-person rounds to reduce cheating opportunities

**H1 2026: Addition, not replacement**
Major companies added AI-assisted rounds while keeping existing formats:
- Amazon redesigned its online assessment to include an AI assistant (January 2026)
- Google piloted code comprehension rounds with Gemini (May 2026, rolled out more broadly by October)
- Meta launched AI-enabled CoderPad rounds with candidate choice of model
- DSA and system design rounds remained unchanged at these companies

**H2 2026: AI fluency as a requirement**
The focus shifted from detecting misuse to measuring productive use:
- Google made AI fluency required for all SWE jobs (October 2026)
- Zapier updated its hiring rubric to grade AI-use slope and accountability (March 2026)
- Companies began reviewing candidate AI transcripts as graded artifacts
- A visible minority dropped coding interviews entirely in favor of AI-native formats (Sierra, some Anthropic teams)

## Timeline: key events

Dates are Pacific time unless otherwise noted. Each entry includes its source tag.

### 2025

**February 27, 2025** – Business Insider reports Amazon's recruiter guidance: "do not use GenAI tools during your interview unless explicitly permitted." Recruiters get a list of behavioral tells including typing while being asked a question and reading answers aloud. [R]

**March 9, 2025** – Sundar Pichai tells CNBC that Google will add in-person rounds. [R]

**May 2025** – Zapier sets a minimum AI-fluency bar for every new hire and publishes its V1 rubric. [P]

**June 11, 2025** – Canva announces "Yes, you can use AI in our interviews." An AI-assisted coding round replaces the CS-fundamentals screen for backend and frontend roles. [P]

**June 12, 2025** – Business Insider reports that Cursor forbids AI beyond autocomplete in screens, then runs a 2-day onsite project. [R]

**July 10, 2025** – Anthropic updates its candidate AI guidance: take-homes are done without Claude and live interviews are "all you" unless indicated otherwise. [P]

**July 2025** – Meta confirms to Business Insider that it is testing AI tools for applicants. [R]

**August 12, 2025** – The WSJ and Axios report that Google, Cisco, and McKinsey are reintroducing in-person rounds over AI cheating and deepfakes. [R]

**November 1, 2025** – Gergely Orosz relays a founder who dropped an AI-allowed round because it only measured "how hands-on candidates were with AI coding tools." [P]

**November 2025** – Interview Coder markets an "undetectable translucent desktop overlay"; it becomes Cluely. January 2026 brings reports of a user-data leak. [P, 2nd]

**Q1 → Q4 2025** – Coinbase's AI-written share of merged code rises from about 5.7% to more than 50%. This is the stated reason Coinbase rebuilt its interviews. [2nd]

**December 2025** – Anthropic's junior postings drop sharply, per its own October 2026 report. [P]

### 2026

**January 7, 2026** – Karat's survey: 62% of organizations still prohibit AI in technical interviews, and 71% say AI makes skills harder to assess. [P]

**January 21, 2026** – Anthropic publishes "Designing AI-resistant technical evaluations." Its performance take-home was redesigned three times because Claude kept beating it. [P]

**January 22, 2026** (approximate) – Amazon's Director of Talent Acquisition Science writes that SDE technical assessments are "reimagined" so candidates "work with an interactive AI assistant on a real coding platform." The post names Amazon Q, Cline, and Kiro as tools its SDEs use. [P]

**February 12, 2026** – CoderPad's State of Tech Hiring 2026: 34% ban AI, 29% allow it with constraints, 17% allow it broadly, and 20% decide case by case. Top signal hiring leaders want: "catches and fixes AI mistakes" (66%). [P]

**February 27, 2026** – Rootly's CEO says take-homes are AI-allowed and graded on the transcript plus a short Loom walkthrough. [P]

**March 3, 2026** – Replit's SRE round runs in a real GCP sandbox with Terraform plus Claude Code or Codex. [P]

**March 31, 2026** (approximate) – Zapier's V2 AI-fluency rubric adds accountability and slope, and redesigns skills tests to watch candidates use AI live. [P]

**April 2026** – Google says about three-quarters of its new code is AI-generated and human-reviewed. [R]

**April 22, 2026** – Sierra's "The AI-native interview" drops its coding and algorithms rounds for Plan, Build, and Review. [P]

**May 2026** – Business Insider reports Google's pilot with Gemini in a "code comprehension" round, run with a handful of orgs. [R]

**May 7–8, 2026** – Ashby announces a voice AI Interviewer, built after acquiring Talent Llama. [P]

**May 12, 2026** – A Cursor engineer (employee #26) says the final stage is a 2-day in-person onsite: pick an idea from Cursor's list, build it end to end, and demo it. [2nd]

**May 13, 2026** – Datadog's AI guidelines: AI is expected in announced AI-assisted coding interviews, forbidden elsewhere, and non-disclosure can disqualify you. [P]

**June 5, 2026** – A Shopify candidate is rejected for using AI "inefficiently." They had used it only to write code "while keeping the logic and thinking to myself." The feedback: "If you can use AI, why not use it to its fullest?" [2nd firsthand]

**July 2026** – HackerRank pitches "Plan, Build, Review" tasks (July 24). Coinbase's first-round AI avatar interviewer "Sophie" is reported (July 13). [2nd, R]

**August 2026** (approximate) – Coinbase publishes "Interviewing Engineers in the AI Era: Lessons from a Year of Rebuilding," discussed on Hacker News. [S]

**August 28, 2026** – HackerRank describes an "agentic development environment" (ADE) interview with a codebase, agentic IDE, terminal, and model choice. [2nd vendor]

**September 2026** – HackerRank's CEO reports repo tasks with an AI assistant at DeepL (Sep 3) and a Wayfair DS/ML AI-assisted interview (Sep 11). [2nd vendor]

**October 14, 2026** – copilotinterview publishes a sourced list of 13 company policies. None allows AI in every round. [2nd aggregator]

**October 16, 2026** – Anthropic publishes "How employment is changing at Anthropic." Scorecards emphasize AI track record, autonomy, and judgment. [P]

**October 20, 2026** – Business Insider reports that Google's AI formats are rolled out "more broadly," still in pilot. AI fluency is required for all SWE jobs, and Gemini is piloted for interview notes and feedback. [R]

**October 27, 2026** – Felix Rieseberg removes the coding interview from his Anthropic team's loops, looking instead for "little prototypes that exhibit taste, empathy, and principled thinking." [P]

**October 29, 2026** – A Reddit OpenAI loop is summarized on X. It includes a 48-hour take-home, a line-by-line drill-down, four onsite rounds, and an experimental round finishing a too-big task with a coding agent. OpenAI's own guide confirms that AI rules vary by round. [2nd, P]

**October 29, 2026** – HackerRank's MCP lets reviewers see a candidate's AI chat history. [2nd vendor]

**November 17, 2026** (upcoming) – Ashby's AI Interviewer launches publicly. [P]

## The pattern

Early 2025 was reactive: companies banned AI and built detection. H1 2026 was additive: big companies added AI rounds and kept DSA. H2 2026 brought three shifts:
1. AI fluency became a written requirement (Google, Zapier)
2. Transcripts became graded artifacts
3. A visible minority dropped algorithm rounds altogether (Sierra, Rieseberg)

## Comparison: early vs. late 2026

| Dimension | Early 2026 | Late 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **Policy focus** | Detecting misuse | Measuring productive use |
| **Common formats** | No AI (default) or optional AI | AI-expected rounds added to loops |
| **Grading** | Completion and correctness | Catches AI mistakes, accountability, slope |
| **Artifacts** | Code only | Code plus AI transcript plus process log |
| **DSA** | Still central | Still central at FAANG; dropped at some startups |

## Common mistakes

- Assuming a company's AI policy applies to all rounds when it varies by stage
- Treating a 2025 policy description as current without checking for updates
- Not asking the recruiter which rounds allow AI and which tools
- Using outside tools in a "provided assistant only" round because the policy wasn't clear

## How to talk about it

"By late 2026, the industry is split roughly evenly: about a third of companies ban AI in interviews, a third allow it with constraints, and the rest decide per round. Companies that added AI-assisted rounds in 2026 typically kept their existing algorithm and system design rounds, so candidates need fluency in both modes. I confirm the AI policy for each round with the recruiter before the loop starts."
